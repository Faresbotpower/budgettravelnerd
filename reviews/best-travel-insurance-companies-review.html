Travel Insurance Buying Guide

What to Consider When Choosing Travel Insurance

Coverage Types You Need

Medical Coverage: Essential for international travel where your regular health insurance may not apply. Look for at least $100,000 in coverage, with $500,000+ for adventure activities or destinations with expensive healthcare.

Trip Cancellation/Interruption: Protects your non-refundable trip costs. Choose coverage equal to your total trip investment, including flights, hotels, tours, and other prepaid expenses.

Emergency Evacuation: Critical for remote destinations or adventure travel. Medical evacuation can cost $100,000+, so ensure adequate coverage.

Baggage Protection: Covers lost, stolen, or delayed luggage. Consider your electronics and valuable items when determining coverage needs.

Key Factors to Evaluate

Pre-Existing Condition Coverage

If you have medical conditions, look for policies that waive pre-existing condition exclusions when purchased within 10-21 days of your initial trip deposit.

Adventure Sports Coverage

Standard policies often exclude adventure activities. If you plan to ski, dive, climb, or participate in other adventure sports, verify coverage or purchase specialized policies.

Age Restrictions and Pricing

Premiums increase significantly with age, and some providers have age limits. Seniors should focus on companies with strong senior coverage and no age caps.

Financial Stability

Choose insurers with strong financial ratings (A.M. Best rating of A- or better) to ensure they can pay claims during major events affecting many travelers.

Customer Service and Claims Process

Read reviews about claims experiences and ensure 24/7 emergency assistance is available in your destination. Look for companies with streamlined online claims processes.

When to Buy Travel Insurance

Purchase insurance as soon as you make your first trip payment to maximize benefits like pre-existing condition waivers and cancel-for-any-reason coverage. Some benefits have time-sensitive eligibility requirements.

What's Typically Not Covered

  • Pre-existing medical conditions (unless waived)
  • High-risk activities and extreme sports
  • Pregnancy-related issues (unless complications arise)
  • Mental health and substance abuse issues
  • Travel to countries under travel warnings
  • Losses due to war or terrorism (unless specifically covered)
  • Preventable circumstances or risky behavior

Tips for Getting the Best Value

  1. Compare Multiple Quotes: Prices and coverage can vary significantly between providers for similar benefits.
  2. Consider Annual Plans: If you travel frequently, annual policies often provide better value than individual trip coverage.
  3. Read the Fine Print: Understand exclusions, deductibles, and claim requirements before purchasing.
  4. Document Everything: Keep receipts, take photos, and maintain detailed records for potential claims.
  5. Contact Your Provider Early: Notify your insurer immediately when issues arise during travel for the best support and claim outcomes.
